What a sample is for

A finish you cannot judge on a screen

Plating is a physical property. Shiny 24K gold, antique gold and rose gold look nearly identical in a photograph and completely different in the hand, because one reflects a room and the other absorbs light into the recesses.

This is why the sample comes before the tooling decision. A finish kit lets you hold twelve options in the same afternoon, put them against your own artwork, and mark the ones that work. It is the cheapest way to settle a question that would otherwise cost a mould to get wrong.

What a sample can be

Four kinds of sample, for four different questions

Most buyers need one of these. If you are not sure which, order a finish sample kit and decide from there.

01

Single piece

One pin in one finish and one size. The fastest way to check whether the plating and the colour are right before you commit to a run.

02

Finish sample kit

The same design across twelve plating finishes. This is the standard choice when the artwork is settled and only the finish is open.

03

Size and colour proof

Several sizes of one design, or one size with the colour count stepped up. Useful when you are deciding how much detail the artwork can carry.

04

Pre-production sample

A small batch made on the actual production run, with production tooling. The last check before the full order is released.

Cost and timing

What a sample costs and how long it takes

Sample pricing depends on the process, the size, the number of finishes and whether a mould has to be cut for it, so it is quoted rather than published.

Quoted, not listed
The sample cost is stated in the quotation with the reasoning, because it depends on what has to be cut and plated for it.
Credited against production
Sample cost is credited against the production order when you proceed, so you are not paying twice for the same piece.
Tooling on a sample
Where a sample needs a new mould, the tooling charge is quoted separately and also credited when you go into production with the same design.
Existing finishes
A single piece in a finish we already run needs no new tool and is usually confirmed and produced within a few business days.
New finishes
A finish outside our twelve standard options needs a plating trial first, which adds time. We will tell you before you pay for it.
Shipping
Samples ship by courier, DHL or FedEx, and the freight is quoted at cost. Express options are available when the programme is already live.

The sample order

What happens on our side

Four stages, and only one of them needs anything from you.

Stage 1 · You

Tell us the design, the finish and the size

Artwork or a photograph, the plating finish from the range, and the diameter. If you are undecided on size, ask for two and pick later.

Stage 2 · We

Quotation and sample confirmation

You receive the sample cost, the production timeline, and the shipping options. Once confirmed, the sample order is placed.

Stage 3 · We

Produced and photographed

The sample is made on the same lines as production, inspected, photographed under daylight and shipped. You get the tracking number with the invoice.

Stage 4 · You

Approve, or adjust and re-sample

If the sample is right, approve it and we release the production order. If it is not, tell us what changed and we adjust the specification rather than guess.

Honest limits

What a sample settles, and what it does not

Samples are the most reliable check in the process, but they are not a photograph of the finished order. Here is the difference, stated plainly.

What a physical sample can and cannot confirm before production
Point A physical sample tells you A render cannot
Plating Exactly how the finish reflects, tarnish resistance and how the colour sits under it Flat colour approximation only
Weight Whether the piece feels right in the hand or on a uniform Nothing at all
Colour accuracy The real match against your Pantone reference under daylight Screen colour, which is not the colour you receive
Artwork detail Whether a fine line survived plating, and how the border reads Whether the line is too fine to fill
Backing Which clutch suits the fabric, and how firmly it holds Nothing at all
Packaging How it presents in the bag or box it ships in Nothing at all

One thing a sample will not tell you

A single sample proves the specification. It does not prove the batch. Large runs are produced over several days on different racks, which is exactly why every piece in your order is inspected individually and why the inspection report travels with the shipment.
