Materials · Base metal

Lapel pin materials: brass, iron, steel and zinc alloy

The base metal is the first decision on a pin order and the hardest one to change later. It sets how thin a line your artwork can carry, how the surface takes a plating, and which processes are open to you at all. Five metals cover everything we run.

Why the metal comes first

Metal thickness sets the floor on your artwork

Metal thickness sets the floor on line width, and the way the shape is formed sets whether the detail is pierced or raised. A metal line narrower than the sheet it is stamped from does not survive the press. It tears.

Our thinnest base metal for enamel and printed work is 0.3 mm brass, 0.3 mm iron or 0.4 mm stainless steel. That is where the 0.3 mm minimum line width comes from. Die struck and 3D die cast hold slightly different geometry, because the relief is formed rather than pierced.

So the metal is not a finishing decision. It is the decision that decides how much of your design survives production.

Two notes on the solid metals

Die struck metal is the most durable option we make, with no enamel to chip or crack, and the visual interest comes from relief depth and the contrast between polished and sandblasted surfaces. Die cast zinc alloy carries multi-level relief from 1.5 mm upward and supports spinners, hinged and rolling elements, which makes it heavier than a stamped pin of the same size.

Common questions

What buyers ask about base metal

It follows from the artwork. If the design carries a gradient or a photograph, it is offset printed on a stainless steel or brass blank. If it needs moving parts, internal cutouts or real depth, it is cast in zinc alloy. If it will be handled hard and you want all metal, it is die struck in solid brass, bronze or iron. If none of those apply, it is hard or soft enamel on 0.3 mm brass, 0.3 mm iron or 0.4 mm stainless steel.
