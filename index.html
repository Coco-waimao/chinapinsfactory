Five production routes

Pick the process the artwork actually wants

The same logo behaves completely differently in hard enamel and in die struck metal. Here is what each route does, what it holds, and where it stops working, so you order the right one the first time.

01

Hard enamel

Enamel is overfilled, kiln fired, then stone-ground flush with the raised metal borders. The result reads as glass over solid metal and carries the highest perceived value of any enamel finish.

  • Minimum metal line0.18 mm
  • Thickness1.5 to 2.5 mm
  • Best forRetail, awards, heritage
Hard enamel details
02

Soft enamel

Plated first, then filled with Pantone-matched liquid enamel into the recessed cavities and oven cured. It holds finer linework than hard enamel and carries a distinct tactile, embossed feel.

  • Minimum metal line0.12 mm
  • Thickness1.2 to 2.0 mm
  • Best forVolume promo, events
Soft enamel details
03

Die struck metal

Stamped under hydraulic presses into solid brass, bronze or iron with no enamel at all. Contrast comes from a mirror-polished relief sitting against a sandblasted or antique recessed background.

  • Relief2D flat or 3D sculpted
  • FinishesAntique, brushed, polished
  • Best forHeritage, heraldry, service
Die struck details
04

3D die cast

High-pressure zinc alloy casting for artwork that needs deep multi-level relief, internal cutouts without pierce marks, or a working mechanism such as a spinner, a track slider or a bobble.

  • CutoutsCast into the mould
  • MechanismsSpinner, slider, hinge
  • Best forMascots, collector series
3D die cast details
05

Offset printed

Printed onto a laser-cut blank and sealed under a non-yellowing polyurethane dome. Full colour gradients and photographic artwork reproduce faithfully, and no steel tooling is needed, so it carries the fastest turnaround.

  • Resolution1200 DPI offset lithography
  • ToolingNo steel mould required
  • Best forLogos, deadlines, gradients
Printed pin details
06

Specialty finishes

Glitter enamel, glow-in-the-dark pigment, translucent stained glass, rhinestone accents and dangling charms. Built for trading pin sets, sports programmes and collector series where the finish is the point.

  • Enamel typesGlitter, glow, translucent
  • ExtrasRhinestone, charm, epoxy dome
  • Best forTrading sets, collector editions

Six workshops under one roof

Vertical integration you can actually audit

Controlling every stage in house is the only reason we can hold a colour across a 50,000-piece run, turn a rush order in five days and tell you exactly who cut the mould.

Tooling workshop

22 CNC engraving centres cutting hardened SKD11 steel to 0.01 mm, with EDM finishing. Moulds are archived for three years so reorders never start from scratch.

Stamping and die casting

16 hydraulic friction presses rated to 200 tonnes for crisp brass and iron stamping, alongside 8 hot-chamber zinc alloy machines for complex cutouts and heavy coins.

Grinding and polishing

Coarse, medium and fine stations run by artisans with more than a decade on the floor, keeping hard enamel flush without cutting through the metal lines.

Licensed plating

A licensed in-house line for real gold, silver, nickel, black nickel, rose gold and antique finishes, with XRF testing to confirm every rack is lead-free.

Enamel colouring

24 dispensing robots handle bulk colour accuracy, with 18 hand-colouring specialists taking over the micro recesses the machines cannot reach.

Inspection and packing

Every piece is checked under daylight LED magnifiers for enamel dust, plating pits and loose posts before it reaches a backing card and an export carton.
