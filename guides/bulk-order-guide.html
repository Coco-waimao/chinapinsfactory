Why the factory asks about quantity before anything else

Quantity decides three things at once: which process is worth using, how the work is scheduled, and how it is packed. It is not a filter for whether we will accept the order. We have no minimum order quantity, and a single piece is a legitimate order for a sample. We ask because the answer changes the specification we quote.

A 25 mm hard enamel pin and a 25 mm 3D die cast pin cost very different amounts to produce, and a hundred of them are produced very differently from ten thousand of them. Understanding which side of that line your order falls on is the first thing a good supplier establishes.

Quantity and scheduling

What changes as the quantity changes

These bands describe how we plan and pack work at different volumes. They are planning guidance, not order thresholds, and there is no minimum order quantity. A one-piece sample is treated with the same care as a large run.

Volume rates apply automatically

There is no separate discount negotiation to run. The rate applied to your order is calculated from the quantity you order, and the quotation states it plainly so you can see exactly which band it landed in.

Lead time is a scheduling window

7 to 10 business days is the standard production window after sample approval. A rush is confirmed against our own capacity, not promised by default, so it is quoted as a fact rather than an aspiration.

Freight scales with volume

Air freight is priced by chargeable weight, so a larger order is more cost-efficient than a small one. For very large runs, sea freight becomes the sensible option and we will tell you when that becomes true.

The full workflow

From sketch to shipped cartons

Nine stages, in the order they happen. Most of them are ours to handle; the ones that need you are marked.

Stage 1 · You

Send the artwork, or just an idea

Vector files in AI, EPS, PDF or PNG are ideal, but a photograph of the physical item or a rough hand sketch is genuinely enough to start. Our in-house designers vectorise from whatever you have.

Stage 2 · We

2D and 3D CAD proof inside 12 hours

You receive a flat artwork proof and a rendered 3D view of the pin in the finish you selected. This is free and carries no obligation. Most corrections happen at this stage, where they cost nothing.

Stage 3 · We

Itemised quotation

The quotation breaks the cost down by tooling, production, plating, packaging and freight, so nothing is a bundled figure you have to guess at. It arrives with the CAD proof.

Stage 4 · We

Mould cutting

Once you approve, the steel mould is cut in our own tooling shop. Offset printed pins skip this stage entirely, which is why they have the shortest lead time we run.

Stage 5 · We

Sample production

A physical sample is produced on the same tooling as your production run, so what you approve is exactly what gets made. The sample cost is credited against the production order.

Stage 6 · You

Sample approval

Check the plating, the enamel colour, the metal finish, the clutch and the size in your hand. This is the last cheap moment to change anything, so we recommend taking it seriously.

Stage 7 · We

Mass production

7 to 10 business days after approval. Stamping, polishing, plating and enamelling run in sequence on our own lines, so a delay in one stage is visible to us rather than hidden behind a sub-supplier.

Stage 8 · We

Inspection

Every piece is inspected under daylight LED magnifiers for enamel dust, plating pits, colour variance and loose posts. A run that fails inspection is reworked, not downgraded into a cheaper carton.

Stage 9 · We

Packing and dispatch

Cartons are counted by two people and sealed against the order. Dispatch is by DHL, FedEx or UPS with the commercial invoice, packing list and certificate documents included.

Tooling and sampling

What the mould actually costs you in time

Tooling is the part of a pin order that takes real time. Understanding it in advance means you can plan around it instead of being surprised by it.

Mould types

  • Stamping mould for hard enamel, soft enamel and die struck pins, cut in hardened SKD11 steel
  • Die casting mould for 3D pieces, in two parts, allowing internal cutouts and moving parts
  • Print plate for offset printed pins, prepared per design with no steel mould involved
  • One mould per design and size, so a second size needs a second mould

Revision policy

  • Free 2D and 3D CAD artwork, returned within 12 hours
  • Revisions at the proof stage are part of the free artwork service
  • Changes after the mould is cut mean a new mould, so we recommend settling the artwork first
  • Moulds are archived three years, so a reorder uses the original tool

Sample policy, stated plainly

The CAD proof and the quotation carry no charge. A physical sample is produced so you can verify the finish before committing to a run, and the sample cost is credited against the production order when you proceed. Samples are available from a single piece, so you can check a plating finish or a clutch type without committing to anything else.

Before the mould is cut, most buyers order one physical sample so the finish and the clutch are settled. The samples and prototyping page sets out the four kinds, what each one costs and what it can and cannot tell you.

Packaging

Five ways to pack pins

Packaging is a real cost and a real decision, so we ask about it early rather than choosing it for you.

Single polybag
Every pin bagged individually. The default for bulk distribution and the cheapest option per unit.
Blister on a backing card
A retail-ready card per pin. Suitable for shelves, and for programmes where the pin is sold individually.
Header bag with your card
Your own printed header card in a sealed bag, with your branding and barcode.
Gift box
Presentation box with a fitted insert. Used for sets, collector series and premium gifting.
Bulk carton packing
Packed loose into export cartons for your own distribution centre or fulfilment partner.

For distributor programmes we can blind drop-ship, so the cartons carry your branding and none of ours.

Payment and shipping

Payment nodes and trade terms

These are the two areas where importers most often get surprised, so both are set out in the open before an order is placed.

Payment structure

  • Deposit to release the mould cut and the production slot
  • Balance after inspection passes and before dispatch
  • Common methods include bank transfer, and other arrangements can be agreed for established accounts
  • Sample orders are settled in full before dispatch
  • The exact terms appear on your quotation, not as a general condition

Trade terms

  • EXW means you collect from our workshop door. Rarely the best choice for a first order.
  • FOB means we deliver to the named port or airport and handle export clearance. The usual choice for buyers with their own freight forwarder.
  • CIF adds sea freight and insurance to the port. Useful when you want a single landed figure but prefer to clear the goods yourself.
  • DDP means we deliver to your door with duties paid. The simplest option for smaller buyers who do not have a customs broker.
  • Air versus sea depends on your delivery date more than your budget, because air freight also compresses the schedule.
Customs and import

Who is responsible for what at the border

This section exists because import responsibility is regularly misunderstood, and misunderstandings here are expensive.

The importer of record is you

Unless we agree a DDP arrangement, the importer of record is the entity that receives the goods in your country. That entity is responsible for customs declaration, duty payment, and any product-specific compliance requirements in the destination market.

Tariff classification is yours to confirm

Harmonised system codes for metal badges and novelty pins vary by jurisdiction and by how the goods are described at the border. We provide a suggested classification and the material composition of what we made, but the final determination sits with your customs broker or your own classification review.

Product compliance is destination-specific

Requirements for lead content, phthalates and finish emissions differ between markets. We test plating racks with XRF to confirm lead-free plating and provide the documentation we hold. Any destination-specific testing your market requires should be confirmed with your broker before you order.

Declared value must match the commercial invoice

The invoice value, the declared customs value and the payment you made should agree. Discrepancies between them are the most common cause of a shipment being held for inspection.
