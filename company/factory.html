Why in-house matters

The machine that cuts your mould is the same building as the people who pack your carton

Buying from a factory and buying from a trading company look identical on a quotation. They are not identical in practice. Here is what changes when the production is in one place.

Colour holds across the whole run

Holding a colour on a 2,000-piece order and on a 200,000-piece order is the same problem: the same pigment in the same enamel, mixed in the same way, checked against the same reference. With one enamel room and 24 calibrated dispensing robots, a reorder two years later lands on the same colour as the original run.

Rush orders are a scheduling decision, not a negotiation

When a trading company is asked for a rush, it depends on a third factory that may already be full. When we are asked, it depends on whether our own presses and plating line have a window. Our 5 business days rush option is confirmed against our own capacity before we offer it.

You know which mould made your order

Every design gets a mould cut in our tooling shop and archived for three years. A repeat order from the same file runs on the same tool, so the second batch matches the first without a re-tooling conversation.

Quality problems get fixed, not passed on

When a piece fails inspection it goes back to the bench that made it. There is no third party to hide behind and no shipment to send while the issue is discussed.

Inspection and packing

Every piece is checked under daylight LED magnifiers for enamel dust, plating pits, colour variance and loose posts before it reaches a backing card and an export carton. A run that fails inspection is reworked, not shipped.

  • 100% piece by piece inspection
  • Daylight LED magnifier bench
  • Plating pit and colour checks
  • Clutch pull test on every back
  • Export carton packing
Quality control

What we check, and when

Inspection is not a final glance at a finished carton. It happens at each handover between workshops, so a defect is caught by the people who can still fix it.

Quality control checkpoints across the production process
Checkpoint What is inspected Method
After stamping Blank shape, relief depth, edge burr, planarity Visual against the approved sample, plus a thickness check
After polishing Surface scratches, dull areas, metal line definition Visual under daylight LED magnifiers
After plating Coverage, thickness, lead content, colour consistency XRF tester on every rack, plus visual comparison to the standard panel
After enamelling Fill level, colour match, dust in recesses, bubbles Visual against the approved sample under magnifiers
After assembly Clutch fit, post alignment, backing card condition Pull test on every back, visual on every card
Before packing Count per carton, carton marking, product mix, label accuracy Two-person count and seal, recorded against the order

Pieces that fail any of these checks are reworked or re-made. They are not separated into a cheaper carton and shipped quietly.
